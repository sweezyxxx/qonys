# Interview 1

**Interviewer:** Hey, thanks for joining us. So, we're working on a website called QONYS. It's basically a platform where people can find apartments in new buildings. And we also want to understand how developers work with apartment information. Is it okay if I take some notes?

**Participant:** Yeah, sure. That's fine.

**Interviewer:** Great. Nothing too formal. Just tell me how things usually work at your company.

**Participant:** Okay, no problem.

**Interviewer:** So, let's start with your job. What exactly do you do?

**Participant:** Well, I'm a sales manager at a development company here in Astana. I've been working there for almost two years now.

Mostly, I talk to customers, answer questions about apartments, explain prices, and help people choose something.

**Interviewer:** Okay, so you work directly with buyers?

**Participant:** Yeah, pretty much every day. I also check which apartments are available or already reserved.

**Interviewer:** And do you manage information on the website too?

**Participant:** Not directly. We have a marketing team for that. But if I notice something wrong, like an old price or incorrect apartment status, I tell them.

**Interviewer:** Okay, and how does that work? Like, imagine you have a new building. How does all the information get to the website?

**Participant:** Hmm, usually we have an Excel file with all the apartments. It has apartment numbers, floors, areas, prices, and statuses.

Then our marketing team takes that information and adds it to the website.

The floor plans and building pictures usually come from our architects or designers.

**Interviewer:** So everything is in one place?

**Participant:** No, that's actually one of the problems. Prices are in one file, plans are in another folder, and pictures are somewhere else.

Sometimes it's a bit messy.

**Interviewer:** Can you give me an example?

**Participant:** Yeah. Let's say we have apartment 45 on the sixth floor. In Excel, it's easy to find. But the floor plan might have some strange file name, like "floor6_final_v2."

Then someone has to open it and check if it's actually the correct plan.

**Interviewer:** Sounds like extra work.

**Participant:** Yeah, exactly. Especially when you have hundreds of apartments.

**Interviewer:** Okay, and what about prices? Let's say an apartment gets reserved or the price changes. What happens?

**Participant:** Usually, the sales department updates our apartment list, and then the information needs to be updated on the website.

But sometimes there's a delay.

**Interviewer:** Has that caused any problems before?

**Participant:** Yeah, actually. I remember one situation when an apartment was already reserved, but the website still showed it as available.

A customer called and asked about it. I had to explain that it wasn't available anymore.

**Interviewer:** And what did he say?

**Participant:** He wasn't very happy. He said something like, "But it's still available on your website."

And, well, he was right.

**Interviewer:** How did you handle it?

**Participant:** I apologised and offered him another apartment on a similar floor. But he didn't really like that one.

So, yeah, we probably lost that customer.

**Interviewer:** What would help avoid situations like that?

**Participant:** I think prices and availability should be updated in one place. And the website should show the new information quickly.

**Interviewer:** Let's say your company starts selling apartments in a new building. What information do you need first?

**Participant:** First, basic things. Building name, location, number of floors, completion date, and some general information about the project.

Then we need the apartment list, prices, floor plans, and pictures.

**Interviewer:** Is all of that usually ready at the same time?

**Participant:** No, not really. Sometimes the sales information is ready, but the marketing team is still preparing pictures.

Or we have the building pictures, but some apartment plans are not final yet.

**Interviewer:** So what do you do in that situation?

**Participant:** We just wait or prepare what we can.

I think it would be convenient if we could add the information step by step. Like, save the project as a draft and publish it when everything important is ready.

**Interviewer:** Would you want to check it before publishing?

**Participant:** Yeah, definitely. Especially apartment numbers and prices. Those mistakes can cause real problems.

**Interviewer:** Okay, let's talk about customers. What do they usually ask about apartment prices and finishing?

**Participant:** Oh, finishing is a common question.

A lot of people ask about WhiteBox or rough finishing. Some customers don't really understand the difference.

**Interviewer:** Yeah, I've heard those words too. How do you explain them?

**Participant:** I usually explain what's included. Like, what has already been done and what the customer still needs to finish.

Because just saying "WhiteBox" doesn't explain much.

**Interviewer:** And can customers choose a different finishing option?

**Participant:** Depends on the project. Some buildings have only one option. Others let you choose.

**Interviewer:** Does that change the price?

**Participant:** Yeah, sometimes quite a lot.

And that's another thing. People see one price online, but they don't always understand that finishing or furniture might cost extra.

**Interviewer:** So how would you prefer to show it?

**Participant:** I think just show the total price for each available option.

Like, this is the price without finishing, and this is the price with finishing. Simple.

**Interviewer:** And who usually prepares the building images and floor plans?

**Participant:** The architects make the original plans, and our design team prepares the images for the website.

**Interviewer:** What about those interactive plans where you can click on a floor or an apartment?

**Participant:** Yeah, I've seen those. They're useful, actually.

You can choose a floor and see which apartments are available.

**Interviewer:** Is it difficult to prepare something like that?

**Participant:** I don't do it myself, but I think connecting all the apartments correctly takes time.

And someone definitely needs to check it.

**Interviewer:** Why?

**Participant:** Well, imagine you click on apartment 35 and it opens apartment 36. The customer might not even notice the mistake at first.

Then they call us about the wrong apartment. It's confusing for everyone.

**Interviewer:** So you'd want to check the clickable areas?

**Participant:** Yeah. If the system could help prepare them, that would be great. But I still think someone from our team should check them before publishing.

**Interviewer:** Okay, let's say someone finds an apartment on your website and sends a request. What happens next?

**Participant:** Usually, we receive their phone number, and one of our managers calls them.

Sometimes we also receive messages through WhatsApp.

**Interviewer:** And do you know which apartment they're interested in?

**Participant:** Not always. That's actually annoying sometimes.

We get a phone number, call the person, and then have to ask what building they're looking at and which apartment they liked.

**Interviewer:** What information would you want to receive?

**Participant:** At least the building name, apartment number, floor, and price.

If they selected a finishing option, that would be useful too.

**Interviewer:** And how do you keep track of all the requests?

**Participant:** We have our own system and sometimes use spreadsheets. But it's not always convenient.

It would be nice to see which requests are new and which ones someone has already answered.

**Interviewer:** Has it happened that two managers called the same person?

**Participant:** Yeah, a few times. It's not a big disaster, but it doesn't look very professional.

**Interviewer:** Okay, now let's imagine you're using our website. You need to add a building, check its floor plans, and answer a customer request. Where would you start?

**Participant:** Hmm, okay. I'd probably open something like "My Developments."

Then I'd choose the building I want to work with.

**Interviewer:** Okay, and what would you do next?

**Participant:** I'd look for a way to upload apartment information. Ideally from Excel, because we already have all that data.

Then I'd add the building pictures and floor plans.

**Interviewer:** And how would you check the plans?

**Participant:** I'd choose a floor, click on a few apartments, and see if the apartment numbers and information are correct.

**Interviewer:** What about customer requests?

**Participant:** I'd open the requests section and check the newest ones first.

It would be good to see which apartment each customer selected.

**Interviewer:** Is there anything that's unclear or missing?

**Participant:** Hmm, one thing. I would want to know whether a change becomes public immediately or if I have to publish it again.

Because if I just change one apartment price, I don't want to go through ten different steps.

**Interviewer:** Anything else?

**Participant:** Yeah, uploading many apartments at once. If I had to enter 200 apartments manually, I probably wouldn't use it.

**Interviewer:** Okay, last main question. What would make your team actually want to use a platform like this?

**Participant:** I think it has to save time. That's the main thing.

If we can update prices, upload apartments, and manage customer requests in one place, that's useful.

But if we have to do the same work in two different systems, people won't like it.

**Interviewer:** What about features like 360-degree views of the building or courtyard?

**Participant:** I think they're good for customers. Sometimes people call and ask what the courtyard looks like or what's around the building.

If they could see it themselves, that might save us some questions.

**Interviewer:** Would it be difficult for your team to work with?

**Participant:** Well, depends. If we can just upload ready 360-degree pictures, that's fine.

But if we need to build some complicated 3D model for every project, that's probably too much work.

**Interviewer:** And what would be the biggest reason not to use QONYS?

**Participant:** Probably if it's too complicated. We already have enough work.

I don't want to spend half my day just updating the website.

**Interviewer:** Okay, I think that's everything. So, from what I understood, one of your biggest problems is keeping apartment information updated, especially prices and availability.

And the second thing is that you want a simple way to upload information and manage customer requests without doing extra work. Is that right?

**Participant:** Yeah, exactly. That's pretty much it.

**Interviewer:** Is there anything else we missed?

**Participant:** Hmm, maybe mobile support. Sometimes I'm not at my computer, so checking requests from my phone would be useful.

**Interviewer:** Yeah, makes sense. Thanks a lot for your time.

**Participant:** No problem. Good luck with the project!
